DoRA

Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation splits each pretrained weight into a magnitude and a direction, then trains the magnitude directly while adapting the direction with a low-rank (LoRA) update. At a matched parameter budget it tracks full fine-tuning more closely than LoRA. All numbers below are real from this folder's demo.

NVIDIA, 2024 arXiv 2402.09353 Magnitude + Direction Parameter-Efficient FT

The decomposition

DoRA rewrites a weight matrix as a per-output magnitude times a normalized direction, and updates them separately (paper Eq. 3).

W = m · ( V / ||V|| ) (decompose the frozen W0: m0 = ||W0||, V0 = W0) W' = m · ( V0 + ΔV ) / || V0 + ΔV || ΔV = (α/r) · B·A (LoRA on the direction)

LoRA, by contrast, has no separate magnitude: it must fold both "how big" and "which way" into the single low-rank term W = W0 + (α/r)·B·A. Giving the magnitude its own parameters is what lets DoRA behave more like full fine-tuning.
