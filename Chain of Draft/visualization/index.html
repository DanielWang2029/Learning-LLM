Chain of Draft

Let a model think step by step — but write each step as a terse draft instead of a paragraph. On a multi-step task, Chain of Draft matches Chain-of-Thought accuracy while emitting a small fraction of the tokens. Everything below comes from one tiny LM trained on CPU in this folder and prompted three ways.

Xu et al., 2025 arXiv:2502.18600 Prompting strategy Token efficiency One model, 3 styles

One problem, three ways to answer

The task is a chained single-digit addition mod 10 (running sum). The same trained model is prompted with a style tag and produces the completion below. Notice reasoning helps a lot — but Chain of Draft keeps only the essential arithmetic.

Standard — answer only (no reasoning) Chain of Thought — verbose prose Chain of Draft — minimal drafts

Same reasoning skeleton, fewer words

The idea

Both CoT and CoD walk the identical sequence of sub-results. They differ only in how each step is written — natural-language prose vs. a bare equation. Accuracy comes from showing the steps; cost comes from how wordy they are.

step k: s_k = (s_{k-1} + a_k) mod 10 CoT : "3 plus 5 is 8" → many word tokens CoD : "3+5=8" → only the essentials answer: #s_n

Why direct answering fails

Composing several dependent mod 10 steps in a single shot is hard for a tiny model — there is nowhere to "hold" the running sum. Emitting intermediate results turns one hard problem into several trivial (a+b) mod 10 lookups, which the model solves reliably.

Standard: S 3+5+2+9 = #? (compute all at once → weak) CoT/CoD : show s_1, s_2, s_3 first (each step easy → strong)
