Direct Preference Optimization

Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model. DPO optimizes a policy on preference pairs with a single classification-style loss — no reward model and no reinforcement learning. Reproduced on a tiny LM aligned to a known "sort the tokens" preference.

Rafailov et al., 2023 arXiv:2305.18290 DPO loss Implicit reward

Two roads to the same objective

RLHF fits a reward model, then does RL against it. DPO shows the RLHF optimum has a closed form — so you can skip both and train the policy directly.

RLHF (InstructGPT-style)

SFT π_ref→ Reward Model→ RL (PPO)→ π
Two extra models + an unstable sampling loop.

DPO (this paper)

SFT π_ref→ Reward Model RL→ DPO loss→ π
One model, one supervised loss on preference pairs.

The insight & the loss

The LM is secretly a reward model

The RLHF objective (maximize reward with a KL leash to π_ref) has a closed-form optimum. Inverting it expresses the reward in terms of the optimal policy:

π*(y|x) ∝ π_ref(y|x) · exp( r(x,y) / β ) ⇒ r(x,y) = β · log( π*(y|x) / π_ref(y|x) ) + β·log Z(x) The policy IS the reward (up to a per-prompt constant that cancels in any preference comparison).

DPO loss Eq. 7

Substitute that implicit reward into the Bradley-Terry preference likelihood. The partition function cancels, leaving a simple loss:

L_DPO = − log σ( β · [ (logπ_θ(y_w|x) − logπ_ref(y_w|x)) − (logπ_θ(y_l|x) − logπ_ref(y_l|x)) ] ) y_w = chosen (preferred), y_l = rejected implicit reward: r̂(x,y) = β · (logπ_θ − logπ_ref) margin = r̂(x,y_w) − r̂(x,y_l) ← pushed up by training

Training dynamics (live from the demo)

As DPO trains, the loss falls, and the implicit reward of the chosen response is driven above the rejected one — the margin grows steadily positive.
