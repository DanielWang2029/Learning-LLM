Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models

Train language models of many sizes on the same data and their losses fall on a straight line in log-log space — a power law L(N) = (Nc / N)^α. This page plots the real losses measured by the demo in this folder.

What the fit says

L(N) ≈ (Nc / N) ^ α_N (paper Eq. 1.1) taking logs: log L = α·log(Nc) − α·log N = b − α·log N ← a straight line

The paper measures α_N ≈ 0.076 for real LMs across seven orders of magnitude. Our tiny CPU reproduction recovers a comparably small exponent on a synthetic corpus — the point is the shape: a clean straight line.

Why bigger is predictably better

The scaling law lets you extrapolate: measure a few small models, fit the line, and read off how much loss a much larger model would reach — before paying to train it.

  • The exponent α_N is the slope of the log-log line.
  • N counts non-embedding parameters (paper §2.1).
  • The relationship is smooth over many orders of magnitude — no sharp thresholds.

Counting N (paper §2.1)

N ≈ 12 · n_layer · d_model² (transformer blocks) embeddings & the output un-embedding are excluded from N

Each model's N is computed exactly in src/tiny_lm.py (num_params), then paired with its measured loss.
