The three-stage pipeline

Human demonstrations are noisy and expensive; reliable comparisons are cheaper. That gap is exactly what RLHF exploits — the RL policy can surpass the quality of its own SFT training data.

The math, one card per stage

Reward model loss §2.3

Bradley-Terry / logistic loss over a preference pair.

loss(φ) = − E[ log σ( r_φ(x, y_chosen) − r_φ(x, y_rejected) ) ] x : prompt y_chosen : the response the labeler preferred y_rejected : the other response σ : logistic sigmoid
  • Only the difference of rewards is supervised — the RM learns a relative ordering, not absolute values.
  • Comparisons are easier to collect reliably than gold demonstrations.

Policy objective §2.3

Maximize reward while staying near the SFT policy (KL penalty).

maximize E_{y∼π}[ r_φ(x, y) ] − β · KL( π(·|x) ‖ π_SFT(·|x) ) REINFORCE gradient (used here): ∇J = E[ (R − b) · ∇ log π(y | x) ] R = r_φ(x,y) − β·(log π − log π_SFT) b = running-mean baseline (variance reduction)
  • The KL term is a leash: it stops the policy from drifting into reward-hacking gibberish.
  • best-of-n is the RL-free cousin: sample n, keep the RM's favourite.
