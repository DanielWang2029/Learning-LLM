GPT-4 · Predictable Scaling

GPT-4 is a closed model — no weights, architecture, or training data were released. The one methodological claim in the Technical Report that anyone can reproduce is predictable scaling: fit a power law on small models and use it to predict a much larger model's loss before training it. This page shows that experiment run end-to-end on a CPU.

OpenAI, 2023 arXiv 2303.08774 Scaling Law: L(N)=aN⁻ᵅ+E Closed model — documented technique only
Honesty note. Nothing here reproduces GPT-4's actual weights or capabilities. It reproduces the documented, verifiable methodology — predictable scaling and capability-vs-scale tracking — with tiny GPTs on a synthetic language, so the mechanism is fully transparent.

The methodology, end to end

Exactly the recipe the report describes: fit on the cheap models, extrapolate to the expensive one.

Step 1
Train a family
Small GPTs of increasing size N on one fixed synthetic language; record each validation loss.
Step 2
Fit a scaling law
Fit L(N)=a·N⁻ᵅ+E on the smaller models only.
Step 3
Predict
Extrapolate the fit to the large, held-out model — before training it.
Step 4
Verify
Train the large model and compare predicted vs. actual loss.

Why a power law?

The scaling law

L(N) = a · N^(-alpha) + E N model size (non-embedding params) E irreducible loss (data's own entropy) a,alpha reducible term fit on small models
  • E is the loss even an infinite model can't beat — the entropy of the source itself.
  • The reducible part falls as a straight line on a log–log plot, which is what makes extrapolation reliable.
  • OpenAI fit this on models using up to 10,000× less compute than GPT-4 and hit the target.

Multiple-choice scoring

score(choice) = mean_t log p(token_t | context) answer = argmax_choice score(choice)
  • Each question: one true continuation of the language + several distractors.
  • The model picks the completion it finds most likely — the same length-normalized cloze scoring used by real MC benchmarks.
  • Emergent-looking capability, measured transparently at tiny scale.
