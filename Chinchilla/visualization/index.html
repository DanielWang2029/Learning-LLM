Training Compute-Optimal LLMs — “Chinchilla”

Given a fixed compute budget C ≈ 6·N·D, how should you split it between model size N and training tokens D? Too big under-trains; too small is capacity-limited. The best model sits in the middle — this page plots the real U-shaped frontier from the demo.

Why the ends are worse

  • Too large: the budget buys too few tokens/steps — the model is under-trained.
  • Too small: even with lots of data it lacks capacity — it is capacity-limited.
  • Just right: N and D balanced. Chinchilla's headline: scale N and D roughly equally.

Why it changed practice

Earlier large models were over-sized and under-trained. Chinchilla showed that for a fixed budget you often want a smaller model trained on more tokens. A 70B model trained Chinchilla-optimally beat much larger models — the same lesson this toy U-curve teaches in seconds.
